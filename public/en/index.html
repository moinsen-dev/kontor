An open, not-for-profit professional network – if it’s needed

Everyone complains about LinkedIn. Everyone stays. Why?

Before anyone builds a “better LinkedIn”, we want to know what really bothers you, what LinkedIn has actually done for you, and whether you would switch. Nine short questions, anonymous, about three minutes. The results are public.

Why I’m asking

I’ve been on LinkedIn since 2014 and active since 2024. I can’t name a single project or connection that really moved me forward. Through XING, the German network, I got a lot of work between 2015 and 2024. Is it just me?

Ulrich Diedrichsen, software developer from Hamburg

What about the alternatives?

  • Polywork raised more than 40 million dollars and shut down in January 2025. Read.cv closed in May 2025. Neither failed on design; they failed because too few people were there.
  • Open professional networks already exist, for example vutuv (open source, connected to the Fediverse) with a few thousand members. Software is not the problem.
  • XING, the German-speaking network, has removed its groups and is used less than LinkedIn, but for many small and mid-sized businesses it is still a place to find work.

So why does everyone stay?

The research points to four reasons. The first two are well documented, the last two are our assessment.

  1. That’s where the people are. Clients, former colleagues, recruiters. A network is only as good as the people in it.
  2. You can’t take your contacts with you. You can export a list, but not the attention, the trust and the recommendations.
  3. Hoping for the one big hit. A single project or job outweighs a lot of frustration. People who succeed talk about it; people who get nothing stay quiet. So LinkedIn looks more successful than it is for most.
  4. People don’t leave, they go quiet. Many cancel Premium, filter their feed or keep their profile only as a business card.

Whether that’s true we’ll only know once enough people answer honestly. Hence this survey.

What this is – and what it isn’t

  • Open: The code of this site is public on GitHub under the AGPL-3.0 licence. The results are public. Questions, criticism and improvements are welcome as issues or pull requests.
  • Planned as not-for-profit: If a network comes out of this, it should belong to its community, not to investors. The legal form (for example an association or a cooperative) is still open.
  • Ask first, build later: If hardly anyone would switch, we won’t build anything. That is a result too.
  • No product, no sales: Nothing is being sold here, and no data is sold or used for AI training.
